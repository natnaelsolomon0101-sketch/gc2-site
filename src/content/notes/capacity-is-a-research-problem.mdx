Every strategy has a size at which it stops working. Most find that size the expensive
way, after the capital is committed and there is nothing left to do but exit into a
market that has noticed.

We underwrite market impact before we underwrite edge. That ordering is deliberate and
it is not how most research gets done.

## Why the usual order fails

The usual order is: find a signal, establish that it predicts something, then ask what
it costs to trade. The problem is that by the time you reach the third question you are
invested in the answer. A signal you have worked on for months acquires a constituency,
and constituencies negotiate. The impact estimate gets generous. The capacity number
gets rounded up. Nobody lies; the number simply drifts toward the one that lets the
work continue.

Asking first removes the incentive. Before a signal is developed we establish what the
relevant market will absorb, at what participation rate, with what expected slippage,
and what all of that does to the return at the size we would actually want to run. If
the answer is that the edge disappears at any size worth having, the research stops
there and the time is saved.

## Capacity is not a constant

The second mistake is treating capacity as a property of the strategy. It is a
property of the strategy, the market, and the moment. Liquidity that exists in calm
conditions is precisely the liquidity that is not there when you need to reduce, and a
capacity estimate drawn from median conditions is an estimate of the wrong thing.

Positions are sized against the market we would face while exiting under stress, not the
one on the screen today. That number is uncomfortably smaller. It is also the only one
that has ever mattered, because the day capacity is tested is never an average day.

## What this rules out

Plenty. Several strategies that work beautifully in research do not survive the
capacity question, and we do not run them. That is not a loss. A portfolio made of
positions that cannot be exited is one that will eventually be exited for you, at a
price someone else chooses.
