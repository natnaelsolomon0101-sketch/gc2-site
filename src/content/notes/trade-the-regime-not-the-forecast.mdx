A point forecast is a claim about a number. A regime is a claim about a state. The
first decays in days. The second tends to survive quarters, and that difference is
most of why we stopped writing forecasts down.

The trouble with a forecast is not that it is wrong. Everything is wrong. The trouble
is that a forecast gives you no instruction about when to stop believing it. A number
that missed by a little looks like a number that is about to be right. So the position
stays on, and the reason it stays on quietly changes from the original thesis to the
fact that it has not worked yet.

## What a regime gives you that a forecast does not

A regime classifier maps the cycle into a small number of discrete states. Not a
continuum, not a score. States. Growth accelerating with liquidity ample is a
different world from growth accelerating with liquidity draining, and the same asset
behaves differently in each.

The useful property is the exit condition. A position sized to a state comes off when
the state changes. That is an observable event, external to the P&L, and it does not
care whether the trade has made money. We are never in the position of arguing with
ourselves about whether a loss is information or noise, because the question we ask
is not "is this working" but "is this still the same world."

## The discipline it imposes

Committing to states forces you to define them in advance, and defining them in
advance is harder than it sounds. A state that only becomes visible after the fact is
not a state, it is a story. Ours have to hold out of sample on data we assembled
ourselves before they are allowed to carry risk, and several candidates that looked
convincing in-sample did not survive that.

The cost is that we are slow at turning points. A regime confirms after it has begun,
and we give up the first part of every move. We accept that. The alternative is a
framework that turns early and often, which is indistinguishable from noise until
enough cycles have passed to tell, by which time the capital is gone.

Trading the regime means accepting that you will never be early. It also means you
will rarely be the last one holding a position whose reason for existing quietly
expired.
